Stage-Specific Care

Support That Adapts as Alzheimer's Evolves

Alzheimer’s disease is progressive, and what works in the early months of forgetfulness will need to change as language, spatial orientation, and physical mobility shift. Without adaptive care, families often face sudden crises—such as elopement, nighttime falls, or severe dehydration.

OnPoint’s Alzheimer’s care model is built around proactive clinical staging. Supervised by Risper Murunga, RN, BSN, MPH, our care team continually reassesses your loved one's cognitive baseline, adapting communication strategies, sensory calming routines, and safety interventions seamlessly over time.

"By anticipating the evolving stages of Alzheimer's, we prevent behavioral crises and ensure your loved one feels safe, understood, and cherished every single day."

Progression Framework

How We Support Each Stage of Alzheimer's

Personalized care techniques calibrated to the specific challenges of each disease milestone:

Early-Stage: Autonomy & Cues

Discreet medication reminders, meal preparation assistance, bill-paying and appointment organization, cognitive stimulation, and companionship.

Mid-Stage: Validation & Routine

Validation therapy, late-afternoon sundowning mitigation, gentle hygiene assistance, wandering prevention, and predictable daily rhythms.

Late-Stage: Total Physical Support

Safe mechanical transfers (Hoyer lifts), pureed dysphagia meal feeding, bed repositioning for pressure sore prevention, and incontinence care.

Around-the-Clock Peace of Mind

Awake overnight monitoring for sleep-cycle disruptions, 24-hour continuous live-in care, and respite relief for exhausted family members.

Behavioral Comfort

Non-Pharmacological Behavioral Protocols

Evidence-based approaches to common Alzheimer's behavioral challenges that preserve dignity and calm:

Repetitive Questioning

Responding to the underlying emotion (anxiety, loneliness) rather than the factual question, using soothing vocal tone and engaging tactile photo albums.

Shadowing & Separation Anxiety

Involving the client in meaningful parallel tasks (folding laundry, stirring batter, sorting buttons) to provide a comforting sense of purpose and companionship.

Delusions & Paranoia

Avoiding confrontation, validating feelings of vulnerability, quietly locating misplaced items, and creating a secure, organized environment.

Sensory Overload Management

Controlling ambient noise, television volume, and clutter to prevent sensory overstimulation that leads to catastrophic emotional reactions.
