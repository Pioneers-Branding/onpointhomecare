Urgent Care Response

When Time Is Critical, We Take the Weight Off Your Shoulders

Care emergencies happen without warning: a hospital discharge coordinator calls announcing your parent must leave today, a spouse caregiver is suddenly hospitalized, or a fall leaves your loved one unable to safely walk to the bathroom.

You do not have to navigate this crisis alone. Our Registered Nurse leadership coordinates immediately with hospital social workers, organizes safe medical transportation, prepares the home environment, and places a dedicated, pre-screened caregiver at the bedside.

"In an urgent care crisis, calm clinical competence is the greatest relief a family can experience. We step in with speed, structure, and compassionate reassurance."

— Risper Murunga, RN, BSN, MPH

Urgent Situations We Handle

When Same-Day Care Is Critical

We step in immediately during life's most stressful care transitions:

Hospital Discharge Today

Discharge coordinators require confirmed in-home support before releasing your parent. We provide bedside handover, medication translation, and doorstep nurse reception.

Primary Caregiver Crisis

A spouse or family caregiver has experienced an injury, illness, or severe exhaustion and cannot physically provide daily assistance alone.

Recent Fall & Acute Mobility Loss

An elder has returned home from urgent care with severe soreness, fear of falling, or weight-bearing restrictions requiring hands-on transfer assistance.

Nighttime Wandering & Delirium

Sudden onset of agitation, sundowning, or confusion that prevents family members from sleeping safely without awake overnight clinical supervision.

Rapid Response Protocol

Our 6-Stage Fast-Track Mobilization

From your first phone call to complete bedside stability in hours.

Stage 01

10-Minute Clinical Intake & Triage

Call our hotline. An experienced nurse gathers critical clinical details: medical diagnoses, mobility status, medication schedules, hospital discharge ETA, and required shift hours.

  • Immediate acuity evaluation and risk assessment
  • Confirmation of required care level (RN, LPN, or Certified Care Aide)
  • Transparent hourly rate and shift plan confirmation
Stage 02

Hospital & Bedside Coordination

If your loved one is in hospital, our RN liaises directly with the hospital discharge planner and unit nurse to confirm safe discharge orders and prescription handoffs.

  • Discharge summary and physician orders review
  • Surgical dressing and medical equipment checklist
  • Safe transportation and door-to-door arrival scheduling
Stage 03

Caregiver Dispatch & Doorstep Arrival

A certified, pre-vetted caregiver or registered nurse arrives at your home before or alongside your parent, ensuring a warm, prepared, and safe reception.

  • Assistance with safe vehicle transfer and settling into bed
  • Baseline vital sign checks (BP, SpO2, pulse, glucose, temperature)
  • Immediate comfort, hydration, and pain assessment
Stage 04

Medication Reconciliation & Safety Audit

We review every prescription bottle in the home, eliminate discontinued hospital medications, set up clear dosette organizers, and remove immediate fall hazards.

  • Cross-referencing pharmacy blister packs against discharge orders
  • Bathroom transfer setup and grab bar security check
  • Emergency protocol and physician contact sheet placement
Stage 05

First-Shift Hands-On Care & Family Relief

Our caregiver takes over all physical transfer duties, dignified bathing, nutritious meal preparation, and soothing bedtime routines, allowing exhausted family members to rest.

  • Full assistance with toileting, hygiene, and mobility
  • Continuous fall prevention and delirium monitoring
  • Detailed shift notes logged in our real-time family portal
Stage 06

Next-Day RN Review & Ongoing Care Roadmap

Following the initial urgent shift, our Lead Registered Nurse conducts a formal in-home review to update the care plan and establish ongoing schedules as needed.

  • Comprehensive 360° health and mobility reassessment
  • Flexible adjustment of ongoing shift hours
  • Direct coordination with family physician and community resources
